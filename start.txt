SHIELD: PRODUCT GOAL

Build a compact, independent, Windows-first defense-in-depth product for AI-assisted endpoint attacks, hijacked AI tools, and abuse of supported accounts. Address the intrusion lifecycle through prevention, detection, containment, eradication, and recovery for explicitly named resources, identities, applications, and provider operations. Controls must protect against the same unauthorized effects whether or not an attacker used AI.

PRODUCT SCOPE AND EVIDENCE
- Shield is the security product. Its identity, security engine, and complete protection scope are not defined by any particular AI client or validation workload. Protecting ordinary devices must not require their users to install an AI agent.
- Requirements below describe intended behavior. Candidate mechanisms and diagrams are provisional designs, not validated controls or supported-platform claims. The implementation inventory below distinguishes current prototype code from missing controls and acceptance evidence. Public research and vendor reports inform the design but do not demonstrate Shield's effectiveness.
- Endpoint protection, AI-tool containment, and provider-specific account protection need independent enforcers and evidence, plus an integrated attack-chain test. Success in one does not establish the others. A task sandbox is not a general behavior firewall for every approved application.
- "Whole intrusion problem" means a full-lifecycle, layered program rather than execution admission alone. It does not mean guaranteed prevention of every intrusion, safe intent recognition, every application/account/platform, or recovery of already disclosed data. Unsupported boundaries and residual risks must be visible; no whole-device antivirus, general DLP, or account-wide capability has been demonstrated.
- The intended user/deployer, customer need, acceptable workflow, and product acceptance budgets still need recorded validation. A successful lab demonstration is engineering evidence, not product-market validation.

INTEGRATED PILOT CONTRACT (USER DECISION 2026-10-28)
- Selected approach: first prove one narrow end-to-end profile across one Windows configuration, one AI integration, and one real account/resource provider, then expand coverage. This explicitly supersedes the earlier endpoint-only release decision. Endpoint, AI, and provider tracks are active requirements, not deferred options. Optional Shield-side AI analysis and other platforms remain outside this pilot.
- Intended users remain typical end users; this is a design target, not validated demand. Name the provisioning operator separately from the daily user, including required device/provider administration, consent, licensing, and recovery access. An enterprise-admin lab setup cannot silently become the assumed consumer workflow. Ordinary endpoint protection must still work without an AI agent installed.
- Protection hypothesis: enforce declared application admission, prevent an approved-but-hostile AI worker from exceeding its resource/action grants, and constrain direct remote callers at the selected provider gate. Demonstrate detection, bounded response, and recovery for the covered incident states. No new executable need launch for an AI or account attack; execution control alone cannot satisfy this contract. [2, 3, 31, 33]
- Proposed legitimate task: retrieve one explicitly approved read-only cloud reference, edit one staged local text note, and publish the exact reviewed output to its approved host destination through a separate grant. Name any inference provider separately and approve disclosure of the reference/task content to it. Cloud writes, permission changes, additional references, and arbitrary host actions are not implicit task rights.
- Required forbidden-effect cases include unapproved ordinary launch paths, out-of-grant local reads/writes/execution, credential extraction, unauthorized egress, persistent configuration/tool changes, cross-task impersonation, and out-of-scope provider reads/writes/permission changes. Identify exact assets and identities in R0; canary protection does not certify every Windows credential store or account.
- Admission and operation authorization are distinct. R0 must name decision makers, trust inputs, enrollment rules, defaults for unknown/new/changed code, grant scope, revocation, and offline behavior. Distinguish one artifact from a publisher/product/version set and one task action from all actions of an approved application. Disclose administrative prerequisites and computer-wide effects; a signature, inventory entry, or user click is not proof of benignness. [23, 28]
- Proposed everyday-workflow set: browser/download use, deliberate legitimate installation, approved application launch/update, ordinary documents, the integrated task, reboot/sign-in, and authorized recovery/removal. Validate these with representative users; they are not yet observed requirements or passed tests.
- Initial usability gates: zero manual hash/ACL/policy-file editing by end users in those acceptance workflows, and zero additional Shield prompts for unchanged already-approved application launches. New or changed authority still requires the declared trusted admission path, not an unrestricted "allow everything" escape hatch.
- Still open: exact Windows edition/build/filesystem, AI integration/transport, real provider/tenant and service gate, identity/resource/verb matrix, enrollment/elevation/licensing prerequisites, admission rules, numeric safety/performance/false-block/response/recovery budgets, costs, maintainer, and finite feasibility budget. A provider demonstration requiring unavailable enterprise controls does not establish consumer-account support.
- Completion requires E1, M1, C1, I1, O1, and P0 after their prerequisites. An endpoint-only, agent-only, mocked-provider, or alert-only result cannot be relabeled as the integrated pilot. An accepted pilot is evidence for its recorded profile, not a promise to stop every intrusion.

PROJECT PRE-MORTEM (PROJECTED FAILURES, NOT OBSERVED COMPROMISES)
- "Whole coverage became three unfinished products": an unbounded endpoint/agent/account program never produced one useful result. Correction: choose one configuration/client/provider and run E0, A0, and C0 feasibility spikes before M0 hardening; do not build most of the trusted infrastructure before discovering that the actual AI client cannot use its required interfaces.
- "We kept planning instead of learning": citations, installed tools, and more requirements supplied no enforcement evidence. Correction: each time-boxed engineering step must produce an executable test/run record or a concrete stop/pivot decision tied to its largest uncertainty.
- "We rebuilt something Windows already did": an unapproved canary launch was treated as potential product value without a consumer-native baseline. Correction: compare actual Defender/SmartScreen/Smart App Control states and equivalent native hardening; stop broad implementation if neither protection nor safe-operability benefit is demonstrated. [4, 22]
- "Our definition of unapproved hid the hard problem": preselected fixtures and allowlists did not explain trust bootstrap, legitimate updates, signed malware, or misleading user approvals. Correction: specify the actual rule scope and test deceptive approval scenarios; do not shift malware classification to typical users or dismiss a confused user as a knowingly hostile owner. [23, 28, 29]
- "The demo passed but ordinary users disabled it": a note edit or blocked binary did not exercise everyday installation, updates, friction, and recovery. Correction: representative benign workloads, held-out challenge cases, and an authorized usability pilot must pass declared budgets; do not shrink legitimate work until security looks successful.
- "Shield became the new attack or outage": privileged brokers, global policy changes, and recovery tools increased trusted code and blast radius. Correction: build only components required by the selected gates, separate installation/runtime authority, bound privileged operations and automatic responses, and prove safe recovery before exposing real users.
- "The green badge survived broken protection": one layer passed under a different policy/build, or a test record was mistaken for runtime authorization. Correction: distinguish the frozen deployment profile, live task grants, and per-run evidence; repeat endpoint tests with integration enabled and invalidate affected results after security-relevant drift.
- "The project ended but security obligations did not": updates, vulnerability handling, signing keys, support, and data retention had no operating owner/budget. Correction: release requires a maintainable support/patch/recovery plan; otherwise retain a bounded lab prototype, not an unsupported public security product.
- "Our allow button changed more authority than it described": a publisher rule admitted future files, or a supplemental policy widened a base policy that the UI represented as a one-file exception. Correction: bind the UI to the actual generated rule and policy relationship; show its machine-wide and future-version scope before authorization. [23, 24]
- "The installed policy was not the active protection": deployment success, a policy file, or a running helper was mistaken for effective user-mode enforcement. Correction: reconcile desired and native-reported policy state, options, activation/reboot requirements, and independent effect probes; audit-only, pending, and unknown states are not prevention. [23, 26]
- "Windows blocked Shield itself": development execution did not prove that an ordinary user could download, install, update, and recover the product with native protections intact. Correction: test the actual distribution path and signing compatibility; Authenticode, SmartScreen reputation, native policy authorization, and update metadata solve different trust problems. [9, 22, 23, 27]
- "Rollback became a boot failure": generic recovery language ignored signed-policy removal rules, unavailable signing keys, firmware access, and encryption recovery. Correction: prove the chosen policy lifecycle and owner-usable recovery before adoption; do not equate a VM snapshot with a shippable rollback mechanism. [25]
- "The benchmark credited Shield for somebody else's block": an invalid probe or an already-blocked native path produced zero markers. Correction: retain baseline outcomes and test-validity denominators per claim; demonstrate causation where claiming added prevention, or measure safe-operability benefit without claiming new detection.
- "The approved AI app completed the intrusion": no unknown binary launched, so execution admission passed while existing tools leaked data or changed persistent state. Correction: test a fully hostile enrolled worker, resource/operation gates, allowed-read/egress composition, and restart/re-import, independently of model refusal. [2, 17, 33]
- "Account protection existed only inside our proxy": a direct caller used valid credentials or a previously issued download URL around local controls. Correction: test service permissions and each derived capability, including after revocation; a denied fresh API call does not prove that cached tokens, sessions, or URLs are unusable. [3, 5, 32, 35, 36]

VALIDATION STRATEGY
- E0, A0, and C0 retire endpoint, AI-interface, and provider feasibility risks before M0 hardening. E1/M1/C1 establish layer evidence; I1 tests composition; O1 tests incident handling/recovery; P0 checks authorized benign operation. A compatibility spike or canary denial is not integrated release readiness.
- Keep independent caller/resource/enforcer evidence for each layer, then test cross-layer composition. Exercise later stages under explicit assumed-compromise conditions so an early block does not hide a missing boundary. Preserve native protections; label the assumed entry state instead of manufacturing an insecure production baseline.
- Before acceptance runs, freeze a versioned profile manifest identifying binaries/helpers, policy options, permitted identities and bounded task-principal creation rules, authority lifetimes/dependencies, broker routes and retry settings, provider permission ceilings, task/disclosure/input-handling rules, concurrency/quota scopes, harness/fixture versions, declared variants, and numeric gates. Record remote properties that cannot be pinned or queried instead of inventing assurance. Changed security-relevant profile fields invalidate affected results.
- Keep three distinct records: the profile manifest specifies the deployment/claim contract; a task-grant manifest authorizes one task within its ceilings; a non-secret run record binds a unique run ID to the profile digest, actual identities/grants/epochs, approved snapshot identities/digests, test variant, and observations. Fresh leases/nonces and task instances within the declared rules are expected run variation, not a new deployment profile. New inputs still require the appropriate approval and case record. Neither a profile manifest nor a run record grants runtime authority; retain authoritative fields once and reference their digests rather than maintaining competing copies.
- Standalone endpoint results cannot certify the integrated configuration: installing an AI client/helper or changing its allow rules can create new routes. Repeat ordinary endpoint probes under the final integrated policy. Compare matched workloads/base configurations with explicit Shield/native-hardening differences; do not pool results from incompatible manifests.
- The ARTEX report motivates provider-side authorization, while prompt-injection reports motivate hostile-input/tool boundaries. Neither incident reporting nor matching terminology establishes reproduction or Shield effectiveness. Test the actual selected resources, identities, and effects. [1, 16, 33]
- Policy unit tests, mocks, model refusal, event logs, and successful commands cannot replace actual OS effects, a real model-backed legitimate task, or direct requests to a real authorized provider.

FIRST INTEGRATED WORKFLOW AND DISCLOSURE CONTRACT
- The trusted user selects one regular local text note and authorizes retrieval of one cloud reference. The broker captures bounded, supported-format reference bytes into a host-controlled snapshot before the online worker starts; record tenant/container/object identity, available version/ETag, byte count, and content digest. Disclosure approval binds that snapshot, not an arbitrary future version at the same URL. Re-fetching changed content requires a new snapshot/approval; the provider's resource permission must not be misrepresented as a per-version gate. [32, 35]
- Shield stages only approved note/reference snapshots. The selected AI client edits the staged note, not the original, and cannot turn a reference identifier into a generic URL-fetch capability. Test reference replacement, changed versions, aliases/redirects, and unsupported formats. Host publication separately approves the exact resulting content/diff and destination.
- Online disclosure contract: task instructions, staged inputs, approved reference responses, and generated content must be approved for disclosure to the named model provider. Do not stage local-only data or reusable credentials. This profile does not promise secrecy of approved task content from that provider or correctness of every edit made within granted authority.
- Keep ungranted host files, credential canaries, controller/provider configuration, and a second forbidden cloud resource outside task authority. Provider permissions must constrain the named identities even for direct callers; the local broker additionally constrains task-specific operations. A local grant cannot create a provider-side restriction by assertion.
- A later local-only processing profile needs a separately enforced disclosure boundary. Separate principals or a destination allowlist alone do not prove end-to-end information-flow control. The selected online pilot must not be marketed as local-only, transparent containment of every AI application, or unrestricted document-format support. [7, 17]

INTRUSION-LIFECYCLE COVERAGE
- Maintain one threat/asset matrix linking each relevant intrusion stage to its caller, entry route, resource/action, prevention or detection claim, enforcer, observer, response, recovery, and known gaps. Map relevant ATT&CK tactics and LLM-specific input/tool paths; the mapping is an analysis aid, not proof of coverage or a claim that attacks follow a fixed sequence. [30, 31, 33]
- Initial access and execution: exercise downloads, document/reference content, ordinary OS launch paths, approved interpreters/tools, and AI tool proposals. Application admission addresses covered code; untrusted content must never install tools, create permissions, or become trusted policy merely because an approved application consumed it.
- Privilege, discovery, and lateral access: constrain the enrolled worker's identities, handles, helpers, IPC, and network reach. Test delegated execution, cross-task access, and unauthorized loopback/LAN/metadata routes as applicable; do not silently turn task restrictions into a blanket host-network outage.
- Credentials, collection, and exfiltration: isolate broker secrets and binding keys, constrain source reads and destination operations, and test attempts to place ungranted data in otherwise allowed requests. Approved model destinations do not make all local data releasable. Protection of specific canaries does not establish protection of every real credential store. [5, 7, 10]
- Persistence and defense impairment: cover claimed startup/task/configuration locations, AI tool/provider settings and reused memory, policy/update/evidence tampering, and provider consent/role changes. Select actual mechanisms in R0; test before/after state and restart/re-import, rather than only one process lifetime. [16, 18, 30]
- Impact and recovery: prevent unapproved publication or destructive operations on covered resources, protect recovery material from the hostile workload, and rehearse authenticated restore and re-enrollment. Already disclosed data, completed external actions, harmful permitted content, and untested resources remain explicit residual risks. Detection/response/recovery are separate O1 outcomes, not retrospective prevention claims. [31]

THREATS AND TRUST ASSUMPTIONS
- Attackers using LLMs for exploitation, malware delivery, or credential abuse. Victims need not run AI. Microsoft's 2026-10-21 report describes AI-assisted abuse of already-authorized cloud applications; successful authentication alone does not establish data access or exfiltration. This is vendor incident reporting, not independently reproduced Shield evidence. [1]
- Attackers hijacking legitimate AI tools through malicious files, webpages, tool responses, or persistent state, exploiting excessive functionality, permissions, or autonomy. [2, 18]
- Assume the attacker can fully control the enrolled AI worker, its model/tool outputs, and its inputs, including executing code with that worker's authority. Also test ordinary non-enrolled endpoint paths and direct remote use of the in-scope leaked credentials. The model's refusal, a launcher's cooperation, or routing through Shield cannot be required attacker behavior.
- The endpoint profile must separately identify ordinary delivery/launch paths, caller privileges, policy subjects, protected effects, and enforcement prerequisites. It cannot inherit the agent profile's exclusion of non-enrolled applications while claiming to protect those applications or launch paths.
- Assume the OS, trusted approval/controller/update components, relevant provider enforcers, and policy-owning administrators are initially trustworthy. Pre-existing compromise of those roots is outside the initial profile. List each credential class and identity in the attacker model; do not extrapolate a restricted application credential test to a broader user/admin session. An escape, credential exposure, policy change, or escalation achieved through a covered path is a failure, not an excluded initial condition.
- Protection relies on the selected OS/service boundaries functioning as tested. No guarantee is made against every OS vulnerability, compromised kernel/firmware, or a knowingly hostile owner. Social engineering or confusing admission prompts are not automatically that exclusion: record whether a typical user's mistaken approval expands policy, and evaluate its consequences. Execution admission alone does not constrain harmful behavior of admitted code; broader behavior/resource protection requires a separately enforced profile. [28, 29]

PROTECTION PRINCIPLES
Shield must authorize covered execution, file, credential, configuration, and network/API operations outside the untrusted application/model. Bind authorization to the actual caller, resource, and operation; a trusted executable, valid login, or approved destination is insufficient. Separate processes are useful for privilege separation but need OS-enforced access restrictions or service-enforced authorization to prevent direct bypass. [2, 3]

For agents, prefer narrow, typed tools and independently authorized high-impact operations. Only the trusted host authorization path, within policy ceilings, may create or expand grants. Files, webpages, tool responses, and retrieved memories may affect agent behavior but must not become host authorization. Keep reusable credentials and protected security/provider configuration outside worker control. [2, 17]

Treat allowed reads and allowed uploads as composable disclosure paths. Claim source-to-sink restrictions only for explicitly modeled and tested flows; testing a few canaries is not a general noninterference proof. CaMeL uses controlled execution and capability/data-flow enforcement; its results do not establish that a transparent endpoint wrapper provides equivalent protection. [7]

Application admission answers which code may start; operation authorization answers what that code may do. Keep task intent, resource/action grants, and provider policy ceilings independent of model-generated text. Input separation, prompt filtering, and human review are useful supporting measures, not substitutes for outside-model enforcement or proof that permitted content is harmless. [2, 3, 33]

REQUIREMENTS
- Own policy, detection logic, enforcement integration, and response without depending on another antivirus engine. Native OS facilities and vetted libraries are allowed; preserve existing platform protections, including installed antivirus. Initial lab tracks test enforcement, evidence, and bounded responses, not a general malware-detection engine. Independence is not a requirement to replace or reimplement antivirus.
- Evaluate native user-mode/OS enforcement options before a custom kernel driver. App Control policies affect the whole computer, not just one task; validate feature availability on the selected edition/build and account for effects on other applications. A launcher covers enrolled paths only. [4]
- Verify actual interpreter effects: App Control's "blocked" script event may mean constrained PowerShell execution rather than no execution; an allowed unenlightened interpreter can execute unapproved scripts. File, credential, and network restrictions remain separate. [8]
- Provider protection requires an authorized integration and an identified service-enforced gate for every claimed caller/resource/action, including direct callers on uncontrolled devices. Inventory user/application identities, credential classes, consent, and role changes. Resource-scoped protection of one identity is not account-wide prevention; monitoring and later revocation do not prevent an already completed action. [1, 3, 32]
- Use appropriately restricted OAuth audiences, resources, actions, and sender-constrained tokens where the provider actually enforces them. Local software or a local broker alone cannot constrain independent remote callers using stolen credentials. No Shield account-protection integration is supported; model-inference brokering is not account-wide protection. [5, 10]
- Minimize measured deployment footprint, runtime overhead, and trusted code without weakening required controls. Attribute protection to the actual OS/service mechanism; do not describe native hardening alone as a newly demonstrated detection capability.
- Protect build, installation, policy, and update paths. Select maintained update verification with authenticated/versioned metadata, expiry/freshness checks, separated signing roles, key rotation, and rollback resistance. Specify bootstrap trust, time assumptions, offline/stale-update behavior, and error reporting. Metadata verification does not itself implement safe installation, recovery, or vulnerability response. [6, 9]
- Windows is the initial target. macOS, Linux, iOS/iPadOS, and Android are proposed future targets, not promised equivalent coverage. Each needs its own OS capabilities, privileges/entitlements, distribution/management constraints, and tested protection profile.
- Agent containment is one control within the product, not a substitute for the broader endpoint/account architecture.
- Implement only the policy/configuration helper, restricted AI execution/tool mediation, credential isolation, provider adapter, evidence/status, and bounded response/recovery components the selected profile actually requires. A narrow broker may be necessary; a resident privileged service, custom driver, or generic framework is not automatically necessary. Keep privileged parsing and the trusted code base small.

IMPLEMENTATION AND VALIDATION TRACKS
SHARED PREREQUISITES
- R0 - select provisional Windows/AI/provider candidates, one legitimate task, protected assets/actions/identities, daily user and setup/recovery operators, policy owners, authority lifetimes, supported task/user concurrency, and an authorized lab/feasibility budget. Record safety bounds, owner, review date, and stop criteria before probes. Use feasibility findings to finalize the candidate and actual numeric acceptance budgets before acceptance runs; never relabel exploratory results as acceptance after choosing thresholds to fit them.
- L0 - after R0, provision an isolated disposable Windows instance, authorized provider test resources, revocable test identities/credentials, and an independent direct-call client before enforcement or cloud-policy changes. Disable unnecessary host sharing, exclude production data/credentials, control egress, and verify local restore plus owned-provider cleanup/recovery. Compilation and local snapshots do not establish provider recovery.
- Lab secret custody: retain replay-test tokens, sessions, keys, and capability URLs only in a protected, test-only store accessible to declared lab operators/probe principals, separate from the repository, run records, logs, and routine exports. Set retention/cleanup deadlines; use non-secret lookup identifiers in evidence. After restoring a local snapshot, reconcile actual remote state and credential validity before another run; restoring local bytes does not undo remote grants or revocation.
- M0 - after L0 and feasible E0/A0/C0 results, harden and validate the actual selected interfaces: OS principals/files/IPC/network/process ownership, typed broker authorization, credential isolation, provider permission checks, limits, failure state, and cleanup/recovery. Use deterministic hostile callers against the recorded candidate; a compatible prototype is not a security pass. AppContainer/jobs alone do not establish all resource restrictions. [14, 15, 21]
- Gate dependencies: R0 -> L0; L0 -> E0, A0, and C0; E0 and A0 and C0 -> M0; M0 -> E1, M1, and C1; E1 and M1 and C1 -> I1; I1 -> O1; O1 -> P0. Feasibility may stop the pilot; no later gate compensates for an unmet prerequisite.

NON-AGENT ENDPOINT TRACK (E0/E1)
- E0 - after L0, retire endpoint feasibility and value risks: establish the R0 admission model, actual OS gate, delivery/launch coverage, enrollment/elevation requirements, and realistic allowed workflow. Compare native baselines before building a broad framework. Include approved applications, legitimate new installs/updates, and signed/interpreter/delegation paths; do not define approval solely around easy-to-block fixtures.
- Admission must specify initial trust/bootstrap, policy provenance, actual matching semantics, update/version boundaries, exception ownership, and changed-software handling. For App Control, distinguish its Authenticode/PE image hashes from an ordinary whole-file release digest; publisher and minimum-version rules can authorize more than one artifact. Test an otherwise unapproved file within the same signer/product family to detect unintended rule breadth. Inventory/learning is not trust validation, and an allowed signature or user approval is not malware classification. [23]
- Proposed first mechanism experiment, not a production selection: evaluate App Control for Business on one explicitly selected isolated Windows configuration with existing native protections retained. Exercise a minimal reviewed user-mode policy, ordinary launch paths, effective-state reporting, a legitimate install/update, reboot, and owned-policy removal/recovery. Record feature/prerequisite failures and product value before writing a resident service or general framework. If this candidate fails, choose another bounded experiment or stop; do not silently substitute a launcher. [4, 23, 24, 25, 26]
- Candidate first probe: a harmless unapproved native executable attempts an inert marker effect through declared ordinary Windows launch paths while an approved control application remains useful. This validates only the scoped gate. A pass cannot substitute for representative workloads, challenge cases, or comparison with consumer-native protection. [4, 22]
- E1 - after M0, run paired endpoint cases: a standalone variant with no AI agent installed, and a composed variant with the selected AI/provider helpers and final policy enabled. Neither may require launching the tested application through Shield. Verify ordinary/alternative launch effects, legitimate utility, recovery, false blocks, and overhead in each; integration-added exceptions must not invalidate standalone assumptions. A known claimed-path bypass blocks that claim; a launcher-only restriction cannot pass.
- Challenge the declared effect through applicable approved-tool/interpreter use, alternate delivery/launch routes, installer/update paths, changed binaries, and policy tampering. Keep held-out cases distinct from development fixtures. Record out-of-scope routes and whether the remaining protection still addresses the end user's problem; do not quietly narrow a failed claim after observing results.

ACTIVE AI-INTEGRATION TRACK (A0/M1)
- A0 - after L0, use a small real-client/model spike to prove that documented interfaces can support the proposed model/reference routing, external credential handling, helper execution, and termination under lab safety bounds. Record actual requested rights and unsupported hooks before M0 hardening. A mock model or offline converter cannot prove client compatibility; a successful uncontained run cannot prove isolation, and handing broad secrets to the worker cannot count as a compatible design.
- Attempt integration through documented interfaces before considering an adapter or fork. Record authentication, inference/reference transport, helpers, native sandbox interaction, and maintenance cost. Codex CLI is one proposed agent-test workload, not Shield's identity, security engine, or complete product scope. It is not a selected, proven-compatible, or mandatory client; recorded release metadata below is not integration evidence. Never disable required safeguards or broaden access to make the client work.
- M1 - after M0, re-run the real clean task under the hardened candidate and malicious-reference/instruction/tool-response variants. Attempt out-of-grant execution, tool registration, persistent changes, credential extraction, snapshot substitution, and disclosure through allowed/denied routes. Exercise duplicate submissions, stale cached replies, lost responses, and crashes around approval consumption/publication. Include deterministic hostile worker probes even if the model resists injection. Use inert markers and actual Windows effects; publication remains separate. [16, 18, 33]
- A0 records provisional routing, rights, non-secret test-credential identifiers, disclosure approval, safety bounds, and observed compatibility gaps. M0/M1 require the frozen profile, per-run records and task grants, actual numeric limits, OS/build and binary hashes, model ID/settings, and policy/enforcer configuration. Record a resolved model snapshot when available; an unchanged alias does not guarantee unchanged remote behavior.
- Agent enforcers to verify: files/configuration through OS identity/ACLs; helper authority through restricted principals/process policy; lifecycle/resource management through tested OS mechanisms; network through OS-enforced default deny plus task-bound broker access; API semantics/credentials through bounded broker operations. AppContainer or job objects alone do not supply all these controls. Record task-local versus machine-wide changes. [14, 15]
- Agent authority: the trusted host user approves task grants within controller/administrator policy ceilings. An administrator-protected controller owns runtime policy and lifecycle with only necessary privileges; exact identities, ACLs, and setup-helper rights remain blocking design decisions.
- M1 must allow necessary model/reference traffic and approved helpers, without unrestricted shell/HTTP/signing brokering. If any relied-on path cannot be mediated, revise the integration or declare it unsupported; never weaken required controls or retry unprotected.
- A0 and M1 are mandatory: A0 retires interface risk, while M1 validates the hardened integration/grants. Neither replaces E1/C1/I1/O1/P0, proves transparent containment of arbitrary AI products, or validates customer demand.

ACTIVE PROVIDER ACCOUNT TRACK (C0/C1)
- C0 - after L0, test the candidate's exact account type, endpoints, resources/actions/identities, credentials, permissions, observation and recovery APIs under the named operators. Verify eligibility, setup/consent/licensing burden, service ceilings, and usable response authority before M0. If only a differently managed customer can deploy it, obtain an explicit target-profile decision rather than silently redefining "typical end users." No usable gate means a blocked pilot, not a proxy substitute.
- Candidate provider probe: Microsoft Graph Selected permissions can scope an application's access to assigned SharePoint/OneDrive resources, with consent, an explicit resource assignment/role, and the appropriate token all required. Evaluate a read-only approved reference and a forbidden second resource; inventory broader overlapping grants and every claimed identity. This is a lab candidate, not a provider selection, proof of consumer-account eligibility, or protection of unrelated user/admin credentials. [32]
- Verify the actual content endpoint, descendants/versions/alternate paths, and derived capabilities using only the intended grants. Do not copy broad SDK/example permissions into the runtime identity to make a request succeed; success with broader scopes does not prove the Selected ceiling. Graph content retrieval can return a preauthenticated URL needing no Authorization header, and removing file permissions might not immediately invalidate it. Include that path in the threat and revocation model. [32, 35]
- For included human identities, select provider-supported phishing-resistant authentication and session/token controls where applicable. Record actual client/resource/device/licensing limits; MFA strength, application permissions, and token binding are different controls. Do not apply a human-MFA claim to noninteractive application credentials or assume every stolen token is bound. [5, 10, 34]
- C1 - after M0 and C0, use valid in-scope credentials from independent clients outside Shield to prove allowed-reference retrieval and denial of declared forbidden resources, writes, consent/role changes, and bypass routes. Verify actual service responses/state, revocation delay, and recovery; authentication failure alone is not a valid forbidden-action test. Test every identity/credential class claimed by the profile, including broader grants if they are permitted to exist.
- Retain test-issued access/refresh tokens, application sessions, and download/share capabilities under L0 secret-custody rules, then independently probe each before and after the declared revocation action/deadline. Record actual timing and surviving authority even if a fresh authenticated API call is denied. Provider documentation's nominal lifetimes are not measured Shield revocation bounds. [35, 36]
- Validate revocation observations with a live unrevoked control for the same endpoint/flow where feasible. Distinguish an authorization denial from an expired credential, cached response, service outage, throttling, or failed probe. If the cause cannot be distinguished, report that uncertainty. Natural expiry can establish an observed cutoff within its measured bound, but not prove that the response action revoked the capability.
- A leaked credential may still exercise its legitimate provider authority; neither Selected permissions nor DPoP prove user intent for each request. Name that residual exposure, and keep reusable credentials/binding keys outside hostile workers. C1 is a provider/resource/identity result, not an entire-account claim. No provider gate is implemented or validated. [5, 10, 32]

INTEGRATED, OPERATIONAL, AND END-USER GATES
- I1 - after E1, M1, and C1, run the real legitimate workflow end to end and attack-chain variants spanning local launch/tool use, cloud-reference injection, resource/credential access, persistence/egress attempts, and direct provider calls. Verify each required prevention effect and legitimate postcondition independently. Include later-stage assumed-compromise entry cases so an early denial cannot conceal a missing downstream gate; preserve original claims and native controls.
- O1 - after I1, rehearse detection, authorized response, eradication, and recovery with inert incidents, provider outages, stale/revoked grants, partial failures, and interrupted install/update/restart. Measure alert/response/revocation/recovery times, actual remaining authority, user impact, and restored coverage against R0 budgets. No automatic broad quarantine, destructive cleanup, or claim of recalled disclosures is permitted. [31]
- P0 - after O1 and installation/update/recovery safety checks, run a bounded, explicitly authorized pilot of representative benign non-agent and integrated AI/account activity. Measure task completion, false blocks, prompts, grant/status understanding, update/reboot compatibility, recovery, and total cost against predeclared budgets. Approval exercises require separately consented benign simulations; attacks remain in the lab, not on users' production data.

PRODUCT ARCHITECTURE BOUNDARIES
- Shared responsibilities are versioned policy/grants, trusted approval, protected evidence, health, bounded incident response, and authenticated updates/recovery. Share schemas/helpers only where semantics genuinely match; OS, worker/tool, and provider authorization remain different enforcement planes.
- In the existing owned-state inventory, mark each identity, key, grant, enforcer, artifact, and budget as task-owned or shared; record its lifetime, consumers, and cleanup/rotation authority. Do not equate a task lease with ownership of a shared provider identity or machine-wide policy. Normal teardown and exposure response have different scopes; no additional generic resource-management framework is implied.
- The endpoint controller must configure/verify the selected OS gate for ordinary covered paths, not assume all applications pass through an agent launcher. Monitoring and killing after a forbidden effect are not prevention of that effect.
- Endpoint boundary: trusted admission interface -> minimal authorized configuration helper -> native gate for claimed effects -> independent state/effect observations. Define concrete principals and rights in E0; a resident service is optional. Keep untrusted parsing and status display away from policy-installation privileges.
- Do not infer a device is clean from an execution inventory or imply that a new execution policy terminates already-running hostile code. State enrollment prerequisites, existing-compromise exclusions, and pre-existing-process limitations plainly.
- Local policy changes, approval requests, diagnostic inputs, and recovery commands are attack surfaces. Authenticate callers, bound schemas/size/rate, and test malformed, replayed, concurrent, and unauthorized requests. Do not expose generic elevated execution, broad path exceptions, or automatic permission expansion.
- Product enforcement map: ordinary programs -> native endpoint gates -> covered local effects; enrolled AI worker -> restricted OS identity plus typed broker -> approved task/model/reference operations; direct remote clients and broker -> provider-native authorization -> named cloud resources/actions. Each plane reports to protected health/incident handling. The local broker is not a mandatory route for an attacker.
- R0 must connect these maps with concrete identities, trust roots, grant/resource identifiers, evidence, and response ownership. A unified UI must not imply a globally atomic policy update, universal mediation, or a provider gate that does not exist. Reconcile partial activation/revocation explicitly.

- These requirements apply if App Control is the selected endpoint gate; they are not a claim that it is selected, compatible, or implemented. Record the exact policy format, owned PolicyID/BasePolicyID, version, artifact digest, rule scope, update/supplemental signers, and relevant options. Map every Shield admission action to the actual policy change and affected users/code. [23, 24]
- Do not assume a generic default-deny configuration covers ordinary applications: App Control policies restrict kernel-mode binaries by default; user-mode coverage requires the UMCI option. Declare script, Store-app, dynamic-code, managed-installer, and reputation options as applicable. Audit mode logs rather than generally prevents disallowed execution, but is not a universally passive dry run: some script behavior changes, and .NET dynamic-code hardening has no audit mode. App Control evaluates explicit denies before allows; a Shield allow must not be presented as overriding those denies or another base policy's denial. Inventory and test the actual native-policy combination on the target build; never remove a conflicting non-owned policy to make an admission appear successful. [23, 24]
- Journal desired, pending, active/verified, failed, and removal states separately. Reconcile native-reported IDs/versions/active state with expected options, required reboot/refresh, and scoped effect checks. TASK GRANT AND RESOURCE CONTRACT
- Use an immutable, versioned task-grant manifest containing its unique grant ID, schema version, profile/policy digests, issue epoch, task/session and enrolled principal/binary identities, permitted operations/resources/parameters, provider tenant/resource/identity ceilings, approved input snapshot identities/digests, disclosure destinations, numeric quotas/deadline, and authenticated host approval. It cannot widen the referenced profile's ceilings. Keep revocation state separately and check the current lease/epoch at dispatch; never embed live credentials or preauthenticated URLs.
- Default deny; explicit deny overrides allow in Shield's policy language. Do not assume native ACL ordering automatically implements those semantics: verify effective OS permissions. No implicit cross-task grant union, authority inherited from untrusted configuration, or automatic expansion after failure. Required runtime resources are a declared baseline, not user-data grants.
- The agent lab profile permits staged-input reads, staged-output/scratch writes, approved reference retrieval, approved model inference, and enrolled helpers when necessary. Host publication is separate. Map each permitted and forbidden operation to an actual enforcer; a policy evaluator returning "deny" is insufficient if another route remains available.
- A local lease constrains broker use, not independent provider requests. Provider-side permissions, token constraints, and revocation must be evaluated separately; expiring a local manifest cannot invalidate a leaked bearer token at the provider by itself. [5, 10, 32]
- M1 task import/publication supports regular local text files, not arbitrary host trees or file types. Reject unsupported hard-link/reparse, alternate-stream, remote/device-path, and alias cases. Bind trusted selections to opened-object identity/content version; prevent replacement/path-resolution races instead of trusting normalization or check-then-open. Specify rename/delete/recreate rights and verify inherited permissions. [21]
- Sensitive worker approvals bind caller/task, exact operation/resource/parameters, task-grant digest/epoch, expiry, and one-use identifier. A separate short-lived host-only publication grant is issued after worker closure, binding the immutable output digest, destination identity, and expected original version. It references the source task for provenance without reviving that worker's expired authority.
- For broker/publication operations, bind a trusted logical operation ID to the caller, applicable grant/epoch, and exact resource/parameter/artifact digests. Scope any caller-supplied retry key to that authenticated context; it is correlation, not authority, and changed parameters are rejected. Atomically reserve any required one-use approval and applicable local quota in the existing protected journal before the first effect; record not-dispatched, dispatched, confirmed outcome, or unknown states. Duplicate requests may return an authorized existing status/result, not create another logical operation or spend the same approval again. Recheck current caller/grant/epoch before dispatch, retry, or cached-result release; a previous allow is not continuing authority.
- A lost response after dispatch is an unknown effect until reconciled, not a denial or rollback. Permit bounded retries only when the actual operation is idempotent under the tested host/provider contract or there is evidence that the earlier attempt was never applied. Configure and test SDK/client retries, prohibit automatic non-idempotent HTTP proxy retries, and count every attempt against its applicable limits. A local operation ID does not create remote deduplication or an exactly-once transaction; do not automatically replay uncertain publication or other side effects. HTTP method semantics alone are not proof of a particular outcome. [38]
- The approval interface shows canonical resources, destinations, disclosure scope, limits, and exact proposed effects/diff. Labels and action parameters come from trusted state, not model-generated summaries. Changed, expired, revoked, or replayed authorization must be rejected explicitly.

AI SESSION LIFECYCLE AND REVOCATION
- Agent-profile lifecycle: preparing -> running -> quiescing -> closed. Cancellation, expiry, required-enforcer loss, or controller failure enters revoking -> terminated -> closed. Closed means covered local worker authority has been removed and verified; it does not mean external effects were reversed. Retained artifacts become host-only review data.
- Before running, verify installed restrictions, effective access, authenticated broker routing, credentials isolation, numeric quotas, and cleanup ownership. A failed/incomplete preflight refuses launch with an explicit reason; never start first and apply required controls afterward.
- Revocation invalidates task leases/approvals, rejects new and queued operations, cancels in-flight work where possible, closes broker-owned task connections/handles, terminates the enrolled process tree, and verifies termination. Updating a policy file or removing an ACL entry is not sufficient to revoke already-open handles. [21]
- Select/test OS-backed process ownership, controller-loss behavior, and a bounded revocation deadline. Job membership alone does not cover every delegated process-creation route; test breakaway and IPC/service-mediated execution. For this profile, restart must not restore old leases or automatically resume tasks. Reconcile abandoned authority before accepting new work. [15]
- Cancellation is not rollback: a request already accepted by an external service or data already disclosed cannot be recalled. Record completed versus unknown effects explicitly; do not report an interrupted operation as safely prevented without evidence.
- Coordinate local task closure, broker leases, owned provider permissions/credentials, and evidence without pretending they form one atomic transaction. Journal partial failures, stop new affected work, measure provider revocation delay, and verify remaining authority before reporting containment. External in-flight effects may remain unknown.
- Track local leases/processes/handles, issuing keys, refresh tokens, access tokens, application cookies, resource grants, and derived URLs separately where applicable. Rotating a secret or stopping new token issuance is not proof that existing tokens/sessions/URLs stopped working. Record the action, remaining capability, verified cutoff or unknown expiry, and independent post-action observation for each class. [35, 36]
- Remove only task-owned grants/resources; normal closure leaves required shared enforcers and authorized broker-private credentials intact. Their continued existence alone is not a failed local task closure. Cleanup failures produce an explicit degraded/error state and recovery action; do not reuse the affected task identity/workspace or declare local closure while local task authority remains. An exposed shared/external capability is a separate unresolved incident, not something a closed worker or deleted workspace proves contained.

- Each profile must declare its applicable security limits, performance/utility budgets, enforcement owners, and measurement method before live validation. Agent-specific limits below are not evidence of equivalent endpoint or provider-side quotas.
- For the integrated profile, measure installed footprint, policy/application startup, steady/peak RAM/CPU, broker/provider overhead, prompts/false blocks, alert burden, response/revocation/recovery times, and storage/log bounds. Include native controls, AI companions, provider/licensing, signing, support, and recovery in total cost. Before M0 acceptance, assign finite runtime limits, units, scope, enforcers, and breach behavior for elapsed time, processes, CPU/memory, staging, request/response sizes, broker capacity/rate, egress, and model requests/tokens. A0 must report unenforced prototype limits; lab containment is not proof of production task limits, and unsupported required limits block their security claims.
- Declare per-task and shared broker/provider-identity limits, active/queued/in-flight bounds, and the trusted counter owner. Reserve aggregate capacity before each attempt, including SDK retries; concurrent tasks or restarted workers must not reset shared counters or create an unbounded queue. Keep conservative reservations for uncertain usage until reconciled, rather than treating timeouts as free attempts. Reconcile unresolved usage after controller loss; local accounting still does not establish a provider-enforced monetary ceiling.

Report Shield-only cost, the existing workload baseline, and total deployment cost including required companions/models separately. AI STATE AND OUTPUT BOUNDARY
- Treat instruction files, tool/provider configuration, and reused agent memory as persistent attack surfaces. Test the selected agent's actual state across restart/re-import; a fresh process does not reset external state. [16, 18]
- In the agent profile, keep worker state/cache task-scoped. Supply only minimal host-controlled integration configuration, not the user's general agent configuration or credentials. Reused memory/artifacts require explicit admission as untrusted, disclosure-approved inputs, never inherited authority.
- Previewing, importing, or reopening artifacts must not automatically execute content, register tools, fetch embedded remote content, or promote artifact text into trusted task permissions/configuration. Host previews must be passive; preserve these restrictions through controlled re-import. Where downstream consumers cannot be controlled, end the protection claim at export and require an explicit, warned user handoff. [17]
- Declare supported text encodings and render attacker-controlled markup, terminal escapes, and control/bidirectional characters unambiguously as bounded literal review data. Bind review/publication to the same exact bytes; do not silently normalize or strip content between approval and publication. Test misleading content alongside trusted operation labels; passive rendering alone does not establish that a user understood the proposed effect.
- Quiescence requires stopping/draining broker work and verifying that workers/helpers or retained writable handles can no longer mutate exported content; a worker's exit report is insufficient. Close the worker session, then create a bounded host-owned immutable artifact for passive text/diff review. Reject unsupported artifacts.
- Publication must use the separate grant and a tested filesystem/locking/identity protocol that rejects a changed original and prevents target-swap races. Atomic replacement alone is not a concurrency or identity check. If the required guarantees cannot be met, refuse that publication path; do not overwrite concurrent changes or silently export elsewhere.

DETECTION, INCIDENT RESPONSE, AND TRUSTED RECOVERY
- O1 requires a runbook for each claimed incident class: observation source, detection rule/threshold and uncertainty, severity, notification route, allowed automatic actions, required approval, response/recovery owner, and independent verification. Correlate endpoint, broker, and provider events by protected identifiers/epochs; denied requests or model labels alone are not proof that compromise occurred. [31]
- Use a trusted status/approval channel outside the hostile worker. Untrusted text cannot dismiss alerts, authorize response, or supply executable remediation. Record unavailable evidence and clock/ordering limits; no quiet "healthy" fallback or raw-content telemetry upload.
- Preauthorized actions may suspend the affected task, deny new broker operations, isolate its owned artifacts, or revoke specific owned provider grants/credentials. Scope and authenticate each action, bound its impact, and test false-positive handling; high-impact machine/account changes need their declared trusted approval. Do not provide a generic elevated shell or disable unrelated native protection as incident response.
- Exposure of a shared broker/provider identity or signing/binding key affects its full declared authority and dependent tasks/profiles, not just the worker that raised the alert. Stop affected new work, withdraw dependent claims, and use the recorded responder/rotation authority; test both this blast radius and normal teardown's preservation of unrelated/shared state. Do not infer account-wide response powers or report global containment after terminating one worker.
- Verify local termination and every claimed provider/session/capability cutoff independently, using the responder's actual permissions. Failed/slow revocation or unavailable response authority is incomplete containment, not success; show surviving capabilities and stop affected new work. Do not mark an access token, application session, or preauthenticated URL revoked merely because a key was rotated or one fresh request failed. Detection and later revocation are not prevention of prior access. [35, 36]
- Eradication/restore touches only owned or explicitly authorized state, preserves bounded evidence, validates authenticated recovery material, rotates exposed in-scope credentials, and rechecks provider/configuration integrity. If trusted host roots may be compromised, withdraw the profile and use an owner-approved rebuild/re-enrollment path rather than claiming an app cleanup proves the device clean.
- Recovery must not replay old grants, pending destructive actions, or poisoned memory/configuration. Test protected backups/known-good artifacts, restore conflicts, interrupted response, and re-import before resuming; record irreversible or unknown effects and the resulting coverage. [9, 18, 31]

- Record baseline, ownership, and recovery information for every Shield-created ACL, network/application-control rule, service, task identity, provider registration, consent, and resource grant. Journal interrupted local/remote transitions and prevent unintended repeated external actions. Distinguish machine-wide, task-scoped, and provider-scoped changes.
- Routine installation, migration, recovery, and uninstall must preserve unrelated/native controls. Restore only owned settings after checking for intervening changes; report conflicts instead of overwriting them. For agent tasks, withdraw task authority before removing enforcers; for the endpoint, retain the required recovery capability until owned-policy removal is verified. Authorized recovery must not bypass update-authentication or silently lower the trusted version floor; disposable lab snapshot restoration is not proof of production rollback resistance.
- Validate the real first-download/install/update/uninstall path with native consumer protections enabled, including the actual delivery origin/metadata and new artifact versions. A locally copied development executable is not distribution evidence. Trusted-provider code signing is a documented Smart App Control compatibility route, not a universal allow or safety verdict; SmartScreen separately evaluates reputation and can warn on unfamiliar downloads. Do not promise warning-free distribution or require users to disable native protections to install Shield. [22, 27]
- Keep package Authenticode identity, App Control policy/update-signer authorization, and TUF metadata trust roles distinct; verification in one does not satisfy the others. Test certificate/algorithm compatibility across the actual controls: current Smart App Control documentation supports RSA and ECC code-signing certificates, while App Control signer-based file rules document RSA-only support. Signing-key rotation, unavailable signing services, and admission of Shield's own updated/recovery components belong in the lifecycle tests. [9, 23, 27]

Identify the observation source and limitations; an allow/deny decision is not independently observed effect evidence. Logging gaps are unknown, not successful prevention.

Use stable non-secret resource identifiers, bound retention/storage, redact exports, and render untrusted strings passively. Protect evidence from workers; do not claim tamper resistance against an excluded compromised administrator. [35, 36]
- Tests require a coverage matrix linking each operation/resource and bypass route to its enforcer, positive/negative probe, effect observer and trust domain, and recovery test. Specify how reads, execution, writes, and egress are actually observed outside attacker control. Include transient/self-erasing effects and observer loss; an absent final marker or a worker's own failure report cannot prove no effect occurred. Predeclare fixture/trial counts, repeated-run settings, and numeric gates. Required unknown/skipped cases block the relevant claim; canary tests do not establish complete mediation.
- Extend that matrix into the per-run claim/evidence ledger: retain layer/stage, caller and non-secret credential identifier/prerequisites, route/code type, profile/grant/artifact/native/provider-state identifiers, baseline, probe validity, actual effect and observation limits, legitimate outcome, detection/response/recovery result, and attribution. Report attempted, valid, invalid, skipped, and already-native-blocked counts without hiding denominators. A selected attack corpus is coverage evidence, not real-world malware prevalence or general prevention probability.

- The selected integrated pilot requires E1, M1, C1, I1, O1, and P0 after prerequisites, plus operational/support readiness. Endpoint-only success does not meet it. Broader applications, providers, identities, platforms, and effects need new profiles/evidence; no release may claim universal intrusion prevention.
- Compare against actual consumer-native configurations, including Defender, SmartScreen, and Smart App Control where available, plus equivalent native hardening. Record edition/build, eligibility, enforcement/evaluation state, cloud settings, and legitimate workload. Do not infer a typical end-user baseline from the development workstation or disable native protections to manufacture a win. [4, 22]
- Also compare the selected AI client's native controls and the provider's existing permissions, authentication, session, and recovery controls, plus equivalent hardening. Attribute benefit to the actual layer/configuration. A native resource-permission denial is not a new Shield detection algorithm. [2, 32, 34]
- SmartScreen warnings and Smart App Control enforcement are not interchangeable. The current Smart App Control FAQ describes no individual-app bypass; Shield admission must not promise to override a native block while claiming to preserve native protection. Verify current feature behavior rather than relying on obsolete clean-install assumptions. [22]
- Before committing to broad implementation, demonstrate either incremental protection for the target threat or materially safer/simpler deployment, task scoping, explanation, and recovery of equivalent protection. Predeclare measurable criteria with representative benign work, false-block/approval burden, administrative effort, recovery, and total cost.
- Packaging native controls can be a valid product benefit, but is not a newly demonstrated malware detector. If neither security nor safe-operability benefit is established, reconsider the intended workflow/value proposition rather than adding an AI classifier or broadening claims.
- Integrated-pilot completion means versioned installable components and an exact profile manifest, evidence for the actual authorized pilot distribution/install path, reproducible E1/M1/C1/I1/O1 run records and native comparisons, P0 evidence, tested admission/activation/update/response/recovery/uninstall, accurate coverage/privacy/limitations documentation, and a pilot support/recovery owner with capacity. Report which effects were prevented, detected, contained, restored, irreversible, or unsupported. A lab spike is not this deliverable.
- A bounded private pilot and a supported public release are separate decisions. P0 and its safety requirements remain mandatory for integrated-pilot completion; a pilot pass does not establish public-release readiness or authorize public distribution. Public release additionally requires the supported-distribution and ongoing patch/support/vulnerability/key-management obligations above. Keep prototype, pilot, and public-release claims distinct rather than making every feasibility spike a complete product or presenting a successful pilot as maintained protection.

ENGINEERING CADENCE AND STOP/PIVOT RULES
- After R0, the next progress artifact must be the bounded lab/comparison harness and its run record, even if it reports failure. Record the hypothesis, supported configuration, binary/policy identifiers, expected/observed effects, utility, and decision. More citations, tool installation, or longer requirements do not count as a protection milestone.
- This amendment does not complete R0 or authorize live workstation/account changes. Name provisional candidates, the actual user/operator, task, identity/asset/verb matrix, lab safety/effort limits, and stop criteria; provision L0 and run E0/A0/C0 before M0 hardening. Each spike must leave a reproducible invocation/run record, requested/observed rights/routes, a legitimate result and failure control, eligibility/cost constraints, and a continue/change/stop decision. Then freeze acceptance criteria/candidate and test it; do not postpone compatibility to the end or count another document as a protection result.
- Give each feasibility step an owner, review date, and finite effort budget before starting. On expiry, explicitly continue with evidence and a revised bound, change the hypothesis/scope, or stop. Do not convert an unworkable native gate into an unbounded driver, fork, classifier, or multi-platform project.
- Stop the integrated pilot if a required boundary cannot be mediated, C0 lacks a usable real-provider gate, an in-scope bypass remains, recovery is unsafe, workflows/response burden fail budgets, native comparisons show no benefit, or support is unresourced. Preserve failed results and the original claim. An endpoint-only fallback or changed threat/identity scope requires an explicit user-approved contract revision and new evaluation.

READINESS AND BLOCKING DECISIONS
- Selected: an integrated pilot for typical Windows users spanning one Windows configuration, one AI integration, and one real provider, followed by evidence-led expansion. Exact client/provider/identities, usable gates, consumer deployment fit, budgets, and maintainable operating model remain unselected/unproven.
- Active blockers: R0/L0 and E0/A0/C0 feasibility; an eligible actual user/operator and client/provider; endpoint admission and composed policy; distinct profile/grant/run records and protected observers; tested process/approval and credential/capability boundaries; shared-authority ownership, retry/commit and aggregate-budget semantics; version-bound input disclosure; safe lab-secret custody and valid direct-provider/revocation observations; usable response authority and predeclared outcome classes; end-user budgets/native comparisons; pilot distribution/recovery ownership and eventual public-release signing/key/support capacity. Missing decisions block dependent gates, not pure exploratory work.
- Endpoint, AI, and provider coverage are mandatory for this pilot. Optional Shield-side AI analysis and other platforms remain outside it. Unknown or unsupported applications/accounts must be shown as such; they cannot inherit a protected badge from the pilot.
- Current evidence includes prototype software checks, a retained independently observed ordinary-AppContainer loopback failure, and current LPAC canaries with Winsock startup unavailable/no listener acceptance plus successful offline stdio use, not a validated integrated protection path. Native WFP setup/effects, ordinary endpoint enforcement, actual model/provider compatibility/ceilings, independent effects, full recovery, user utility and all milestone acceptance remain unverified. No first-release protection, usability, safe-recovery, or maintenance claim is established by this document.

STATUS
An executable Rust prototype, native App Control/WFP backends, an explicitly opted-in protected read-only verifier implementation, pinned package publisher verification, verified zero-capability LPAC launching and scoped native/CLI tests now exist, but the requested complete Shield program and its integrated protection are not complete or demonstrated. The earlier ordinary-AppContainer loopback bypass is retained; current LPAC probes pass their scoped observations by recording Winsock startup failure/no connection, not complete egress denial, and the offline stdio workflow works. WFP, ordinary endpoint admission and the verifier service have not been deployed/effect-qualified, current native authoring/direct standard-user inventory prerequisites fail, actual provider/model and independent account gates are untested, and no isolated Windows/provider lab or E1/M1/C1/I1/O1/P0 pass exists. Publisher verification does not supply a production Shield signing identity, authenticated updater, protected version floor or deployment/maintenance qualification. Missing authorized lab resources, operators and credentials cannot be replaced with mock success, broader permissions or changed claims. Policy-compliant actions can still be harmful, and completed external effects may be irreversible. No universal, whole-device, account-wide, arbitrary-AI-client, or compromised-kernel/firmware protection has been demonstrated.
